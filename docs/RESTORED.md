# Restored browser characters

The Android 1.1.3 port ships six of the browser game's eleven player characters. OpenWheels
restores the other five in `src/restored/` (marked `// RESTORED (PC addition):` where they hook
into `src/game/`). They are ports of the browser game's Flash v1.87 classes, built as
mobile-style `CharacterB2D` riders and `Vehicle`s.

| id | Character | Vehicle class | Extra controls (keyboard; buttons on touch) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | Lawnmower Man | `LawnMower` | space: deck lift; the blade grinds what it catches |
| 7 | Explorer Guy | `MineCart` | space: clamp onto rails; shift / ctrl: stand up / crouch |
| 8 | Santa Claus | `Sleigh` (with two elves) | space: flight while the boost meter lasts; shift: let go of elves |
| 10 | Irresponsible Mom | `MomBike` (daughter's trailer bike, son's basket) | space: brake; shift / ctrl: eject son / daughter |
| 11 | Helicopter Man | `Helicopter` | space: magnet; shift / ctrl: reel the rope in / out |

The ids are the browser game's character ids. The extra buttons use control bits 0x20 and 0x40
(shift and ctrl on the keyboard). Arrow keys, space and Z work as for the original characters.
`FlashParticles` draws the browser game's debris and spark particles these characters use.
The kids' gore follows the QoL "child gore" option (`docs/QOL.md`).

## Assets

Nothing of the browser game is in the repo. At build time `tools/assets/extract_character.py`
rebuilds each character from the player's own browser-game SWFs (`characters/character<N>.swf`
and the sound SWF `happy_sounds_v1_72.swf`, by default under `binary/flash/swf/`) into the
mobile game's formats:

```
generated/restored/shared/   Characters_restored.plist, character and vehicle body plists
generated/restored/<tier>/   sprite sheets, character-select icons, main-menu portraits,
                             control buttons
generated/restored/sounds/   sounds the mobile assets lack
```

The main-menu portraits (`<tier>/menus/main/portraits/char<id>_portrait.png` and
`char<id>_portrait_25p.png`) are drawn in the mobile portraits' style: a head-and-shoulders bust
cut off by the picture's edges, 1700 x 1880 px on large (the originals are 1480-1988 x
1753-1900; medium 1/2, small 3/8, tiny 1/4, the 25 % version a quarter of each). The script
re-renders the rest pose's upper body at the zoom the bust needs, so the vector art stays sharp.
Files generated by an older version of the script have no portraits; the stamp version (2)
makes the next build regenerate them, and until then the main menu shows the generic portrait
(`PerspectiveCharacters` falls back to it for any missing portrait).

It runs as a post-build step (CMake on Windows, the Gradle task `owGenerateRestoredCharacters`
on Android) and is skipped quietly when the SWFs or its tools are missing (Java 11+, FFDec,
numpy, Pillow; ffmpeg for the sounds). The conversion conventions are validated with
`--calibrate`, which converts Irresponsible Dad's SWF and compares the result with his Android
files.

Without the generated files nothing changes: the character list stays the original six. With
them the five appear in character select, play in any level that lets the player choose, and
stay forced in converted browser levels that require them (`docs/FLASH_LEVELS.md` 10.4).

### "Hide vehicle" starts

A browser level that forces a restored character with "hide vehicle" (`<info h="t">`) gets that
character's own ragdoll without the vehicle, as Flash's `PlayableCharacterB2D`:
`restored::createBareCharacter` (called by `online::createBareCharacter`) builds a plain
`CharacterB2D` from the character's body description, sprites, gore and voice (Flash tags: Char11,
Char2, Santa, Char4, Heli), ejected from the start, controlled with the ejected d-pad and grab.
`PlayableCharacterB2D` creates nothing else: no vehicle, no Irresponsible Mom kids, no Santa
elves, and shift / ctrl do nothing (the ejected controls leave out the kid / elf buttons). The
Explorer is not among Flash's helmeted characters there, so his hat stays on and his head smashes
at the normal limit.

## Restored character campaigns

OpenWheels ships its own campaign for the restored characters: one chapter per character, six
levels each, after the original chapters in the level select and in the main menu's portrait
row. The levels are OpenWheels' own (nothing from the browser game), in the browser game's
level format (Flash editor 1.87 XML), and force their character (`<info f="t" c="<id>">`):

| index | Chapter | Levels (`res/levels/restored/<folder>/`) |
|---|---|---|
| 100 | Lawnmower Man (`06_lawnmower_man`) | Lawn and Order, Suburban Sprawl, The Grass Is Greener, Hedge Maze, County Fair, Mow or Never |
| 101 | Explorer Guy (`07_explorer_guy`) | Temple Run, Mine Shaft, Idol Hands, Rope Bridge, The Lost City, Tomb Raided |
| 102 | Santa Claus (`08_santa_claus`) | Rooftop Run, Chimney Drop, Workshop Chaos, North Pole, Naughty List, Silent Night |
| 103 | Irresponsible Mom (`10_irresponsible_mom`) | School Run, Soccer Practice, Mall Madness, Playground, Traffic Jam, Family Vacation |
| 104 | Helicopter Man (`11_helicopter_man`) | Lift Off, Skyscraper, Crane Game, Air Traffic, Magnet Mayhem, Mayday |

**Files.** `res/levels/restored/chapters.plist` lists the chapters with the structure of
`levelData.plist`'s (`index`, `name`, `comment`, `characterIndex`, `levels` of `dataFile`,
`name`, `locked`, `force_character`, `playable_character`); `dataFile` is relative to `levels/`
(`restored/06_lawnmower_man/01_lawn_and_order.xml`). The build copies the folder to
`levels/restored/` next to the executable (CMake post-build on Windows, Linux and macOS, where it
lands in `Contents/MacOS` beside `generated/`), into the app bundle's resources on iOS, and into
the APK's `assets/levels/restored/` on Android (Gradle task `owStageCampaignLevels`);
`tools/package_windows.ps1` packs it. The game finds it as `levels/restored/...` through the
search path after the player's assets (the exe folder, the iOS bundle root, the APK's assets
root).

**Loading.** `Settings::getAllChaptersData` appends `restored::appendChapters`: only chapters
whose character has been generated (`restored::hasCharacter`), each cut before its first
missing level file (so progress keys never shift). Their level entries get `format = flash`;
`Settings::updateLevelDataFromFile` plays them converted by `online::FlashLevelConverter`
(`restored::playableLevelXml`, the same conversion as online and editor levels; the original
chapters' files are never touched).

**Chapter indices and progress.** The chapters use `index` 100-104, so their progress keys
(`c100_l0`, `c100_l0_unlocked`...) can never meet an original chapter's, whatever the player's
`levelData.plist` holds. 1.1.3 assumes chapter index == position in the chapter array in
several places; those now map between the two (`Settings::getChapterPosition` /
`getChapterIndexAt`): the level-select pages and buttons, the main menu's portrait row,
`advanceLevelIndex` / `advanceChapterIndex`, `UserProgress::getPercentageOfLevelsOfChapterCompleted`
and `getAllLevelsCompleted`. For the original chapters both are the same, so nothing changes
for them.

**Unlocking.** The first level of each chapter is open; every other level opens once the level
before it has a completion time (`unlock_after_previous`, checked by
`UserProgress::isLevelUnlocked` when rewards.plist has no rule for the level; no "level
unlocked" alert). A locked level's alert says which level to complete. QoL "unlock all levels"
opens them all. Completion times persist like the original levels'; "Reset level progress"
clears them too.

**Flow.** Victory NEXT plays the chapter's next level; after the last one the game returns to
the level select, which stamps the chapter as completed and moves on to the next campaign
chapter. The original chapters never lead into the campaign ones (finishing the last original
chapter still ends with the credits, which only count the original chapters), and the campaign
chapters do not count for the credits.

**Races.** A race on a campaign level of these chapters sends the level's XML along (not every
player has the chapter) and every player plays it as an imported level, converted on his own
machine.
